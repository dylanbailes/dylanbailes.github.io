01 / PROBLEM

Study cell response under controlled fields

A stem-cell research lab needed a way to apply repeatable electric and magnetic stimuli to cell cultures and observe the response. The team built a modular platform with four wells so experiments could be controlled separately while microscopy remained available beneath each chamber.

02 / REQUIREMENTS

Make the fields measurable and the samples visible

  • Four optically clear, sealable chambers with individual stimulation control.
  • A magnetic field of at least 1.5 mT with good uniformity across each well.
  • Electric-field control around the 1.5 V/cm target while avoiding electrolysis.
  • Microscope access, fluid ports, and a clear path to field feedback and calibration.

03 / ARCHITECTURE

Four repeatable well modules

Each chamber paired a clear-bottom well and titanium electrodes with Helmholtz coils for magnetic stimulation. A microscope stage sat below each well. The system architecture connected sensor feedback and stimulus control to a Raspberry Pi touchscreen interface; the final integrated build used an ESP32 control path and perf-board drive electronics.

04 / ELECTRONICS

Drive, sense, and debug the magnetic subsystem

I designed two KiCad PCB prototypes for the magnetic-field electronics, including the drive and Hall-sensor feedback paths. PCB debugging exposed issues that could not be fully revised within the project timeline. The working integrated system used perf-board assemblies for field drive while the PCB design remained a clear next iteration.

05 / FIRMWARE

Bring up STM32 control hardware

STM32 firmware development included I²C and UART interfaces, peripheral bring-up, and recovery from option-byte configuration problems. When HAL did not provide the needed behavior, I configured peripherals through direct register access. That work turned board-level problems into specific, testable issues.

06 / SIMULATION

Check field behavior before and after building

ANSYS Maxwell simulations helped compare magnetic-field geometry and predict uniformity. The team also simulated the electric field in the saline-filled wells. Those results guided the prototype geometry and provided a reference for physical field mapping.

07 / TESTING

Measure what the prototype delivered

Bench mapping measured a 4.98 mT magnetic field at the coil center and less than 1% variation across the well footprint. Hall sensors tracked the 100 Hz drive waveform, and AC tests covered the intended 0–250 Hz range. Cross-well magnetic isolation still needs formal characterization.

08 / LESSONS LEARNED

Isolate subsystems, then integrate with evidence

Working coil and field measurements provided a validated baseline even while the custom drive PCB needed another revision. The next engineering steps are to complete that board revision, integrate it into the control system, and characterize interaction between neighboring wells. The project reinforced the value of keeping a testable prototype path when custom electronics lag system integration.
